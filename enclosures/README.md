# Field node casing

One casing for the Poacher Guard field node: a smooth white shell and a blue base plate (the colours of the dashboard).
Inside: ESP32 DevKit V1 and the flame sensor module. On top: the USB webcam. Everything is generated by `make_field_node.py`.

```
pip install trimesh manifold3d shapely mapbox-earcut matplotlib numpy
python make_field_node.py            # writes stl/*.stl and report.json (watertight and fit checks)
```

| File (`stl/`) | What | Colour | Size (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `field_node_shell_print_orientation.stl` | the shell, flipped so the roof lies on the bed | white | 150 x 100 x 40.5 |
| `field_node_base.stl` | back plate with strap lugs, board supports and flame-module post | blue | 150 x 136 x 29.5 |
| `field_node_shell.stl` | the shell as assembled (for viewing) | | |

Assembled body: 150 x 100 x 45 mm, portrait, plus 18 mm strap lugs each side (136 mm wide overall) and the webcam standing on the top end. Needs a print bed of at least 160 x 140 mm (base) . Previews are in `previews/`.

## Features
* **Front (away from the tree):** a round hole through which the IR flame sensor head pokes out 6 mm.
* **Top end:** a camera deck (50 x 26 mm recess) with four zip-tie slots; the webcam stands on it with its lens facing away from the tree.
* **Right side wall:** one 22 x 9 mm port lined up with the ESP32 micro-USB socket (the board lies with its USB end facing it) and eight vent slots. There is no port for the webcam: it plugs straight into the PC and its lead runs down the outside.
* **Back plate (against the tree):** four strap lugs (two each side) with 30 x 5 mm windows for a 25 mm strap or ratchet strap, four rubber-foot pockets to grip the bark, four hidden M3 screws from the tree side.
* **Left side:** seven vent slots. **Right side:** engraved POACHER GUARD wordmark.
* The report checks that the parts do not overlap and that the boards clear every wall (0 mm3 overlap).

## What to buy
* 4 x M3 x 16 self-tapping screws (or M3 x 16 screws with heat-set inserts)
* 4 x self-adhesive rubber feet, 10 mm round
* 4 x cable ties, 2.5 mm wide, for the webcam
* Optional: a silica gel sachet

## Printing
| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Material | PETG or ASA (PLA is fine indoors for the defence) |
| Layer height / walls / infill | 0.2 mm / 3 walls / 20 % |
| Shell | print the `_print_orientation` file, roof on the bed, **no supports needed** |
| Base | flat, no supports |
| Two colours | print the shell in white and the base in blue (a second colour is just a second spool) |

## Assembly
1. Fix the flame module on its post with one M3 x 6 self-tapping screw, sensor head poking through the front hole.
2. Drop the ESP32 onto the four blocks (USB end towards the right side wall). Add a dab of hot glue on one block.
3. Plug in the ESP32 USB cable, lead the ESP32 lead out of the right side port, set the shell over the back plate and fit the four screws from the tree side. Then strap the unit to the trunk, camera end up.
4. Zip-tie the webcam to the top-end deck and let its lead run down the outside to the PC.
5. To stand it up, put it on its bottom end (stick a non-slip rubber strip on that face) and drop about 180 g of steel washers or lead shot into the ballast tray before closing the shell. To mount it on a tree, strap it through the four lugs instead.

## Check before printing (measure with callipers)
* Boards differ a little between makers. Edit `ESP`, `FLAME`, `PAD` and the inner size `XIN, YIN, ZIN` at the top of `make_field_node.py`, then run it again.
* If your webcam is larger than the 52 x 32 mm pad, increase `PAD`. It only sits on top, so any size works with the straps.
* If your flame sensor head points up instead of forward, raise `FLAME["sensor_z"]` or make `SLIT_H` taller.
* Print the base alone first and drop the real boards in.

## Honest notes
* The casing is splash resistant at best (vents, cable port and camera slots are open). Keep it dry, or add a rain hood outdoors.
* Keep the node out of direct sunlight, because the sun gives false readings on infrared flame sensors.

## Assembly animation

`assembly.html` is a self-contained 3D viewer (no internet needed): ten steps, play/pause, scrub, speed, x-ray shell, drag to orbit. Rebuild with `python animation/build_animation.py` after changing any STL. Component models (ESP32 DevKit V1, flame module, USB webcam, cables, screws, zip ties, feet) are in `stl/components/`; they are stand-ins for fit checking, not for printing.

## Standing on its own

The flat bottom end is the foot. The ballast tray (36 x 32 x 18 mm, bottom left inside the base) holds roughly 180 g of steel washers or lead shot, which lowers the centre of mass. Estimated tip angle: about 8 degrees without ballast and about 14 degrees with it, so it stands on a desk but a pull on the cable can topple it. Put a non-slip strip on the end face and keep the cables slack.
